Paywall and onboarding A/B tests that lost

Most teams publish their wins. These are published tests from subscription apps where B lost: higher prices, discounts, longer trials, more plans, a paywall you can’t close, one more onboarding step. Each one is a test somebody already paid for, so you don’t have to.

Tests that lost, with what happened

  • Raise both prices for owners of expensive iPhones ($59.99 → $79.99 a year, $8.99 → $11.99 a week). Conversion 2.55% → 2.09%, revenue per user flat. Glam AI, Adapty
  • Raise the price by 30%. Conversion −5%. Travel app, Adapty
  • Intro offer: a year for $59 against the standing $79. 0 yearly purchases out of 2,500 people. Glam AI, Adapty
  • 50% coupon on the Business plan. Placement revenue per user down 5×. Glam AI, Adapty
  • Extra phone number field before web payment. About 30% fewer people reached payment. web2wave forum
  • Add web checkout next to in-app purchase. Paying conversion 7.8% → 7.4%. RevenueCat
  • Remove the close button so people must start a trial. More trial starts, then more refunds than the gain. BoldVoice, Sub Club
  • Trial 7 → 14 days. Slightly more trials, fewer paid; went back to 7 days. Wellness app, RevenueCat
  • Several prices on the paywall instead of one. Lower conversion; a single weekly price “always won”. GlowUp, Adapty
  • Weekly + monthly or weekly + annual instead of monthly + annual. Both weekly pairs lost on revenue per user and total revenue. Travel app, Adapty
  • A “set a carb goal” step added to onboarding. “Lost quite badly”. Lose It!, Sub Club
  • A red brand-promise banner above the plan cards. Conversion “dropped drastically”. The Economist, FunnelFox

The first six have numbers. The rest come from interviews and case studies that say B lost without a figure. A loss in one app is a warning for yours, not a forecast. They come from a base of 390 published tests, 33 of which lost.

What the losses have in common

  • Trial starts went up, money went down. Forcing a trial or pushing an unclosable offer lifted trial starts, and refunds took it back. Decide on paid or revenue per viewer, not on trial starts alone.
  • A discount next to the full price teaches people to wait. The intro year at $59 sold zero against a standing $79, and a 50% coupon cut revenue per user five times.
  • More choice, less buying. Extra price points and extra plan pairs lowered conversion in more than one app.
  • Every extra field and step costs people. A phone number field before payment lost about 30% of people on the way to it.
  • The same change flips by market. A weekly price rise that worked in Europe failed in the UK for the same company. Read results by country.

Before you run one of these
