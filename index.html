Growth Test Planner: will your A/B test see your change?

A free planner for paywall and onboarding A/B tests in subscription apps, made for product designers and anyone who isn't a statistician. It tells you the smallest change your test can catch, how many weeks to run it, the day you can decide, and which published test ideas are big enough to show up. It also reads a finished test. Everything is computed in your browser; nothing you type leaves the page. The planner needs JavaScript; this text describes what it does.

What it does

  • Plan a test. Four questions: what you test (paywall or onboarding), traffic a week, today's conversion, and the free trial. You get the smallest change the test can catch after 2, 4, 6 or 8 weeks, the start and decision day (with time for the last free trials to bill and refunds to show), people per version, and test ideas ranked by the size of change seen in published tests.
  • Read a finished test. A few numbers per version. Trust checks come first (a fair split, the last trials billed, refunds), then the result with its likely range, where the money came from (more people paid, or people chose pricier plans), and what to do: ship, stop, or test again.
  • Share the plan or the result as a short message for your team, in three tones.

Questions it answers

How long should a paywall A/B test run?

As long as it takes for the change you expect to be bigger than the noise, in whole weeks, plus the longest free trial and about a week for refunds before you decide. Example: 50,000 paywall views a week, 2.75% of views end in a payment, weekly $6.99 and annual $39.99 sold half and half. Judged on money per paywall view, an A/B test catches changes of +12.9% or more after 2 weeks, +9.1% after 4, +7.5% after 6 and +6.5% after 8. With a 3-day trial the decision comes 3 + 7 days after traffic stops.

What is the smallest change my test can detect?

It's the minimum detectable effect: the smallest lift the test can tell apart from chance at 95% confidence and 80% power. It shrinks as traffic and weeks grow. A copy tweak usually moves revenue by a few percent, so a test that only catches +9% won't see it: test something bolder or run longer.

Should I judge a paywall test on conversion or on revenue per view?

Prices, plans or trial changes: money per paywall view, because conversion can fall while revenue rises. Copy and layout changes: paid conversion answers faster.

Onboarding test: trial starts or paid conversion?

Trial starts answer in weeks. Paid conversion is what you care about, but you wait for the trial to end. Example: 20,000 new users a week, 5.8% start a trial: a 4-week test catches +8.0% or more on trial starts.

Why wait after the test stops?

People who start a free trial on the last day pay only when it ends, and refunds show up in the days after. Deciding earlier reads trials as if they were payments.

How do I read a finished A/B test?

Check that the groups split as planned and the last trials billed, then look at the likely range of the lift, not only the best guess. If the whole range is above zero, B won; if it straddles zero, the test can't tell, which is not the same as "no effect".

What should I test on my paywall or onboarding?

The planner lists ideas with the range of results seen in published tests (Adapty's A/B test library, case studies from Superwall, RevenueCat and others), losses included, and marks the ones your test is big enough to catch. They are what other apps saw, not a forecast for yours.

How the numbers work

Per-version sample size n = 2·(z₁₋α/₂ + z₁₋β)²·CV² / MDE² at 95% confidence and 80% power, solved for the change a given number of people can see. Money per view uses the price mix; conversion uses (1 − p)/p. Three versions use a Bonferroni correction. "Prove it's not worse" is one-sided with a 5% margin. Reading uses a chi-square split check, a delta-method range for money per person and a log-ratio range for conversion.
