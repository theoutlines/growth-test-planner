A/B test split: 90/10, 80/20 or 50/50?

An even split is the fastest way to an answer. Every step away from 50/50 costs people: 80/20 needs about 1.6 times as many people (or weeks) to catch the same change, and 90/10 almost 2.8 times. Use an uneven split when B could hurt revenue and you would rather wait longer than risk it.

What each split catches

A paywall with 50,000 people a week (about 214,000 in 30 days), 2.75% paying, weekly $6.99 and annual $39.99 sold half and half. The smallest change in revenue per viewer the test can catch (95% confidence, 80% power):

“People needed” is how many people the test needs, against a 50/50 split, to catch the same change. It works out as (1/A share + 1/B share) / 4.

When to split unevenly

  1. B touches the price, the plans or the trial. A bad price can cost real money for weeks. 80/20 limits the damage; plan the longer run up front.
  2. B changes copy or layout. Little risk. Split 50/50 and get the answer sooner.
  3. You want to protect A but have little traffic. 90/10 on a small paywall often can’t catch anything a design change will do. Test something bolder, or wait for more traffic.
  4. Don’t change the split mid-test. People who arrived under one split and people under another aren’t comparable; restart instead.
