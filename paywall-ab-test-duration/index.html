How long should a paywall A/B test run?

Long enough for the change you expect to be bigger than the noise, in whole weeks, plus the longest free trial and about a week for refunds before you decide. For most subscription apps that is 4 to 8 weeks of traffic, and the smaller your traffic, the bolder the change has to be.

What a test can catch, by traffic and weeks

The smallest change in revenue per paywall viewer an A/B test can tell apart from chance (95% confidence, 80% power), for a paywall where 2.75% of the people who see it pay and buyers split half and half between weekly $6.99 and annual $39.99:

Judged on paid conversion instead, the same paywall catches a little less: 50,000 people a week catch +7.5% in 4 weeks (+9.1% on revenue per viewer). Your own numbers change these figures; the planner works them out for your traffic, conversion, prices and trial.

How to pick the length

  • Start from what you expect the change to do. A headline or button tweak rarely moves revenue by more than a few percent. Making the annual plan the hero has moved revenue per user by +25% to +40% in Adapty's A/B test library. If your test only catches +9%, test the bolder change.
  • Run whole weeks. Weekdays and weekends convert differently; a test that stops mid-week reads that as an effect.
  • Add the trial and the refunds. People who start a trial on the last day pay only when it ends, and refunds arrive in the days after. With a 3-day trial, decide 3 + 7 days after the traffic stops.
  • Don't stop early on a good day. Peeking and stopping the moment B looks ahead turns noise into wins. Pick the length first, then wait.

Judge on revenue or on conversion?

Prices, plans or trial changes: revenue per paywall viewer, because conversion can fall while revenue rises. Copy and layout changes: paid conversion answers faster.
